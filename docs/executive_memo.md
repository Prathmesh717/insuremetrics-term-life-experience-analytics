# Executive actuarial memo

**To:** Mortality and Assumption Governance Committee  
**From:** Experience Analytics  
**Subject:** 2021–2025 term-life mortality and lapse experience  
**Data classification:** Synthetic portfolio demonstration

## Recommendation

Maintain the current portfolio mortality and lapse bases. Place mortality on
enhanced monitoring and perform targeted reviews of TERM10 500K–749K mortality
and TERM30 duration-1 lapse experience. The evidence does not support an
immediate portfolio-wide assumption change.

## Portfolio results

The study contains 63,642.64 life-years. There were 127 approved deaths against
113.91 expected, producing count A/E of 111.5%. Actual death benefits were
55.35 million against 50.89 million expected, producing amount A/E of 108.8%.

The mortality count A/E 95% interval is 92.9%–132.7%. Limited-fluctuation
credibility is 57.5%, yielding a credibility-weighted A/E of 106.6%. Because
the interval includes 100% and the weighted deviation is moderate, the
portfolio is assigned `MONITOR`.

The lapse study recorded 3,006 voluntary lapses against 2,969.82 expected.
Lapse A/E is 101.2%, with a 95% interval of 97.6%–104.9% and full
limited-fluctuation credibility. The portfolio lapse action is `MAINTAIN`.

## Material observations

- TERM10 / 500K–749K mortality recorded 16 deaths versus 7.91 expected. Count
  and amount A/E were both 202.3%; the lower 95% bound was 115.6%. This is the
  clearest adverse mortality diagnostic, but the segment remains small.
- Calendar-year 2025 mortality A/E was 124.1% by count and 129.9% by amount.
  Eleven large claims occurred in 2025, indicating that severity and claim mix
  contributed to the adverse result.
- TERM30 duration-1 lapse A/E was 118.0% based on 221 actual lapses versus
  187.31 expected. The lower 95% bound was 102.9%.
- Portfolio lapse experience remained close to basis despite year and segment
  variation.

## Proposed actions

1. Reconcile the flagged mortality claims to underwriting and claim-detail
   attributes, focusing on face amount, issue cohort, and claim concentration.
2. Investigate TERM30 early-duration lapse by acquisition channel and premium
   characteristics when those fields become available.
3. Refresh the study with the next valuation extract and require the targeted
   signals to persist before updating assumptions.
4. Quantify pricing and reserve impacts before governance approval of any
   segment factor.

## Controls and caveats

All 19 automated data, exposure, expected-value, claim, lapse, and reporting
reconciliations passed. The source data and assumption bases are fictional;
results demonstrate methodology and governance workflow rather than an
actuarial opinion on a real block.
